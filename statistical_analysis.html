Discovering genres by clustering music based on audio features

Python · scikit-learn · PCA · K-Means & Gaussian Mixture Models

An unsupervised machine learning study on whether the 600k+ track Spotify dataset naturally clusters into the human-assigned genres using only Spotify's algorithmic audio features. After dropping redundant features (energy, for its strong correlation with acousticness and loudness) and standardizing the remaining twelve, I applied PCA, K-Means, and Gaussian Mixture Models — using a PCA scree plot, a WCSS elbow plot, and BIC to inform component choices. Comparing cluster assignments to genre labels yielded ARI ≈ 0.015 and NMI ≈ 0.11 (K-Means), with GMM performing slightly worse, indicating that audio features alone do not recover musical genre. The clusters did, however, capture distinct sonic profiles, and a decade-by-decade analysis of the features documented long-term shifts such as declining acousticness and the 1990s "loudness wars" — evidence that genre is largely a social construct rather than a measurable property of the audio itself.

Cancer mortality & poverty rates

JMP · R · Simple linear regression

An examination of a possible connection between poverty rates across the United States and cancer mortality rates. I discuss the validity of the simple linear regression results and look into outlier cases, concluding that poverty rates accounted for roughly 18% of the variability in cancer mortalities across the US, based on the sample used.

North Carolina birth weights

JMP · R · Multiple linear regression

A regression using multiple variables to predict the birth weights of North Carolinian babies, including the mother's minority status, smoking status, and number of previous births. In combination, these variables explained about 46% of the differences seen in birth weights.
