Discovering Genres by Clustering Music Based on Audio Features

Python · scikit-learn · PCA · K-Means & Gaussian Mixture Models

Problem. Music genres are largely a social construct — assigned by culture and marketing rather than measurable properties. But if genre is socially defined, do Spotify's algorithmic audio features still produce natural clusters that align with the genre labels people have assigned?

Approach. Using the 600k+ track Spotify dataset, I dropped redundant features (energy, for its strong correlation with acousticness and loudness) and standardized the remaining twelve. I applied PCA, K-Means, and Gaussian Mixture Models, using a PCA scree plot, a WCSS elbow plot, and BIC to inform component choices. I then compared cluster assignments to genre labels using ARI, NMI, Homogeneity, Completeness, and V-measure, and analyzed audio features by decade to add historical context.

Result. Cluster assignments did not closely align with genre labels: ARI ≈ 0.015 and NMI ≈ 0.11 for K-Means, with GMM performing slightly worse. In other words, audio features alone do not recover musical genre — supporting the view that genre is largely a social construct. The clusters did capture distinct sonic profiles, and the temporal analysis documented long-term shifts such as declining acousticness and the 1990s "loudness wars."
